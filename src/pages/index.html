What the app actually does

Guided pelvic floor sessions

Seven sessions that get harder in a deliberate order, from eight rounds of a 3-second squeeze to fourteen rounds of a 6-second squeeze, each with a relax phase that is at least as long as the squeeze. The ring counts it out so you are not watching a clock and losing the contraction.

The relax half, which most apps skip

A pelvic floor that cannot let go is its own problem, and squeezing harder makes it worse. Every session gives the release its own timed phase and its own cue, because the muscle has to do both jobs.

Breathing and hip mobility

Timed breath patterns for arousal control and stress, plus short mobility routines for the hips and lower back. The pelvic floor does not work in isolation — it moves with the diaphragm on every breath.

A 30-day plan, not a library

One day at a time, three phases, four goals a day. Streaks, a history grid that shows exactly which days you finished, and scores recalculated as you go. No feed to binge and nothing to browse.

What it does not do

VitalityRise is a general wellness app. It is not a treatment, it does not diagnose anything, and it will not tell you that a muscle exercise fixes every cause of erectile difficulty — because it doesn't. Erections depend on blood vessels, nerves, hormones and what is going on in your head, and the pelvic floor is one part of one of those. What the evidence supports is real but specific, and the evidence page says exactly how specific.

There are also no leaderboards, no social features, no ads, and no photographs of anyone's body. The app is deliberately dull to look at over your shoulder.
