What the app actually does

Guided pelvic floor sessions

Seven sessions that get harder in a deliberate order, from eight rounds of a 3-second squeeze to fourteen rounds of a 6-second squeeze, each with a relax phase that is at least as long as the squeeze. The ring counts it out so you are not watching a clock and losing the contraction.

The relax half, which most apps skip

A pelvic floor that cannot let go is its own problem, and squeezing harder makes it worse. Every session gives the release its own timed phase and its own cue, because the muscle has to do both jobs.

Breathing and hip mobility

Timed breath patterns for arousal control and stress, plus short mobility routines for the hips and lower back. The pelvic floor does not work in isolation — it moves with the diaphragm on every breath.

One day at a time, three phases, four goals a day. Streaks, a history grid that shows exactly which days you finished, and scores recalculated as you go. No feed to binge and nothing to browse.

What it does not do

VitalityRise is a general wellness app. It is not a treatment, it does not diagnose anything, and it will not tell you that a muscle exercise fixes every cause of erectile difficulty — because it doesn't. Erections depend on blood vessels, nerves, hormones and what is going on in your head, and the pelvic floor is one part of one of those. What the evidence supports is real but specific, and the evidence page says exactly how specific.

There are also no leaderboards, no social features, no ads, and no photographs of anyone's body. The app is deliberately dull to look at over your shoulder.

Common questions

Is VitalityRise free?

The assessment and your baseline scores are free. The 30-day plan and the guided sessions need a subscription: $4.99 a week, $9.99 a month or $34.99 a year — the monthly and yearly plans both start with a 3-day free trial — or $99.99 once for lifetime access. Prices are US App Store prices and vary by country.

Does it work on Android?

Not yet. VitalityRise is iPhone-only, iOS 17 or later. There is no web version and no Android build in progress.

Do I need any equipment?

No. Pelvic floor training is bodyweight work you can do sitting, standing or lying down, and nobody can tell you are doing it. The app is the timer, the progression and the record.

How long does a session take?

Between one and three minutes. The shortest session is eight rounds of a 3-second squeeze; the longest in the plan is fourteen rounds of a 6-second squeeze. The daily plan usually adds a breathing or mobility session on top, so a full day is under ten minutes.

Will this cure erectile dysfunction?

No, and any app that says otherwise is selling you something. Pelvic floor training has real trial evidence behind it for erectile function — in the best-known randomised trial, 40% of men regained normal function at six months and another 35.5% improved — but it is one input among many, and persistent ED is a reason to see a doctor rather than an app.

Is my data private?

Your assessment answers, scores, streaks and plan progress are stored on your device and are never uploaded. There is no account and no login. The app does send anonymous, hashed usage analytics and privacy-preserving install attribution, which are covered in detail in the privacy policy.

How do I cancel?

In iOS Settings → your name → Subscriptions, or in the App Store app under your profile. Cancelling during a 3-day trial costs you nothing. The support page walks through it.

Before you start

  • Erectile difficulty that has lasted more than three months, or that came on suddenly
  • Chest pain, breathlessness or leg pain on exertion — ED shares its plumbing with the heart, and often shows up first
  • Pain during erection, a curve that is new or worsening, or any pain in the pelvis or perineum
  • A pelvic floor that will not relax, or urinary symptoms — these need a pelvic health physiotherapist, and strengthening alone can make them worse
  • Low mood, low libido or fatigue that arrived together — worth a hormone and mental-health check
