Dr. Kegel alternatives

By Kyrylo Lozovyi · September 18, 2026

Short answer: Dr. Kegel is the biggest men's pelvic floor app by a wide margin — 45,000+ US ratings — and its weaknesses are the ones size produces: eight concurrent price points from $4.99 a week to $59.99 a year, decided by the funnel you land in, and no way to buy it once. If you want clinician-designed, Squeezy Men ($4.99 once, NHS physiotherapists). If you want the cheapest credible timer, Kegel Trainer. If you want a fixed 30-day plan with a lifetime price and no add-ons, ours — with the caveat that we are new and have no ratings. If you want the most users and the most polish and don't mind the pricing, Dr. Kegel is still the answer.

Disclosure: we make VitalityRise, one of the apps below. Every price and rating on this page was read from the app's own US App Store listing on 18 September 2026, no affiliate links, and our own entry lists its weaknesses in the same terms as everyone else's.

Dr. Kegel: For Men's Health is the category leader and it is not close — over 45,000 US ratings against under 10,000 for the next largest. If you are looking for an alternative, it is usually for one of three reasons, and the right alternative depends on which.

Why people leave

  • The pricing. Eight concurrent in-app purchases are listed: a $9.99 "1-week trial", $4.99 weekly, two monthly plans ($12.99 and $19.99), a $29.99 3-month plan, and three yearly plans ($29.99, $56.99, $59.99). That is not a price list; it is the visible surface of a price test, and what you pay depends on the onboarding variant you were assigned.
  • No one-time option. Everything is a subscription.
  • The polish is the product. It is a very well-built consumer app. Some men want something plainer, cheaper, or designed by someone with a clinical background.

The alternatives, by what they do better

Squeezy Men — clinician-designed, one price

$4.99 once. Living With Ltd. 4.62 on 45 US ratings; 4.77 on 1,125 in the UK.

Designed by chartered NHS physiotherapists specialising in men's health, according to its own listing, and built to run a programme a clinician has set — which makes it the obvious choice if you are already seeing a physiotherapist and want the app to follow their plan rather than its own. Its listing also says something no other app here says: that men should only be doing these exercises if they have symptoms or are around a prostatectomy. That is the most conservative position in the category and, given the evidence, not an unreasonable one.

The trade: it is a UK app with a small US user base, and it is a tool rather than a programme. It will time what your physiotherapist told you to do. It will not tell you what to do.

Kegel Trainer PFM Exercises — cheapest, oldest, not men-specific

$2.99/mo, $9.99/6mo, $19.99/yr, or one-time unlocks from $2.99. Olson Applications. 4.84 on 8,196 US ratings.

The lowest credible prices in the category and the longest track record. The trade is that it is a general pelvic floor trainer: nothing in it is aimed at erectile function or ejaculatory control, and the framing is not written for men. If all you want is a reliable timer with progression and you already know the protocol, it does that job for less than anything else.

Kegel Men — the closest like-for-like

$9.99–$19.99/mo, $19.99–$44.99/6mo, $23.99–$59.99/yr, plus paid add-ons. 4.62 on 9,583 US ratings.

The nearest thing to Dr. Kegel in shape and size, which is both the case for it and the case against. It has the same funnel-driven pricing (ten listed purchases) and adds separate paid packs on top of the subscription — a $39.90 "AI" coach and a $19.90 tips pack. If your reason for leaving Dr. Kegel was pricing, this does not solve it.

Built around a fixed 30-day programme rather than an open library, with one price per period, no add-on purchases, and a lifetime option for people who would rather not subscribe. Every session times the release phase as deliberately as the squeeze, because a pelvic floor that cannot let go is its own problem.

The highest yearly price in the category with no lifetime option. Hard to make the case for it over Dr. Kegel on price, or over Squeezy Men on credentials, or over ours on structure. Included because it is often listed, not because we would pick it.

Common questions

Is there a free alternative to Dr. Kegel?

Not a genuinely free men's app — every one in the category is a free download with training behind a paywall, or a small one-time price. The closest to free are Squeezy Men at $4.99 once and Kegel Trainer's $2.99 unlocks. Genuinely free is a phone timer and the protocol on our kegel page, which is what the apps are timing anyway.

What is the best one-time purchase kegel app for men?

Squeezy Men ($4.99, designed by NHS physiotherapists) is the strongest one-time option built specifically for men. Kegel Trainer offers one-time unlocks from $2.99 but is a general pelvic floor app. VitalityRise offers a $99.99 lifetime purchase at the other end of the range, with a fixed 30-day programme behind it.

Why does Dr. Kegel show different prices to different people?

Its listing carries eight concurrent in-app purchases for what is essentially one product, including a $9.99 '1-week trial'. That is what continuous price testing looks like from the outside: the price you are offered depends on which onboarding variant you were assigned, not on a list price. It is not unique to Dr. Kegel — Kegel Men lists ten — but it is most visible on the largest app.

Does any kegel app work better than another?

No app can tell whether you are contracting the right muscle, and the clinical trial that produced the good erectile-function numbers used a physiotherapist and a biofeedback device that none of these apps replicate. The apps differ in price, in whether they train release as well as squeeze, in how hard they upsell, and in whether they give you a plan or a library. They do not differ in what the exercise does.

Is Dr. Kegel worth it?

If you want the app the most men use, with the most polish and the widest feature set, and you are comfortable with subscription pricing that varies, then yes. Its size is a real advantage — it will still exist next year, which is not something a one-person app can promise. The reasons to look elsewhere are price structure, wanting a one-time purchase, or wanting something designed by clinicians rather than by a growth team.

Is VitalityRise a good Dr. Kegel alternative?

It is a fixed 30-day plan rather than a library, has one price per period with no add-on purchases, offers a lifetime option, and times the release phase as deliberately as the squeeze. It is also new, iPhone-only, has no rating history, and is built by one person. Whether that trade is right for you depends on how much you value longevity versus structure.
